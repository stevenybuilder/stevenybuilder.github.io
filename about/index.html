About

I’m Steven Yang. I’m interested in model biology and applied interpretability for models that act in the physical world.

I started by exploring world models: what a model learns about its environment, how that information is represented internally, and whether those representations can be edited. Othello’s model-native coordinate system was an important inspiration.

That led me to vision-language-action models. They offer a concrete way to ask how language and scene information turn into a motor decision, and to test an intervention by watching what a robot actually does.

I’m especially interested in the gap between finding a signal inside a model and being able to control its behavior selectively. I want to understand both the useful interventions and the cases where they break down.

Current work

Changing a robot’s mind investigates instruction following in π0.5 using activation patching, representation analysis, and simulated robot rollouts.
